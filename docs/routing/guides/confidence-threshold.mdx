The **confidence threshold** is the setting that most affects the quality and cost of your router.
It's how confident the router must be that a model can complete a request before that model is
allowed to serve it. The default is **0.80**.

## What it does

For each request the router gives every model a confidence. Models at or above the threshold are in
the confident pool, and the cheapest of them serves the request. See
[how a routing decision is made](../how-routing-works/decision).

- **A higher threshold is more conservative.** Fewer models clear the bar, so fewer requests go to
  cheaper models and more fall back to the default model or escalate. Quality risk drops, and so do
  the savings.
- **A lower threshold saves more.** More models clear the bar, so more requests go to cheaper
  models, with more risk that a cheaper model isn't up to the task.

## How to tune it

1. **Start at the default (0.80)** and send representative traffic.
2. **Look at the routing reasons** in [Activity](./monitor-and-debug). A large share of
   `default_no_confident` means the threshold is high for your catalog, or that your catalog
   doesn't cover this traffic. Lowering the threshold helps in the first case, and adding models in
   the second.
3. **Check quality, not just cost.** Compare outputs against your current setup on a sample of
   your own requests. See [run a trial on your traffic](../evaluate/run-a-trial).
4. **Change it in small steps** and re-check. In the router's **Configuration** tab, use **Edit
   configuration**.

## What the threshold doesn't do

It doesn't change how models are ranked once they're in the confident pool. That's decided by price,
expected output length, cache state and effort. See
[what the router sees](../how-routing-works/what-the-router-sees). It only decides who gets into the
pool.
