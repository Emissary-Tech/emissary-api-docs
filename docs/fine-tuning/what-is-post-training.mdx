**Post-training** is how you adapt a base model to your specific task. Prompting only changes what
you put *into* a model. Post-training changes the model's **weights**, which gives you more stable,
precise and differentiated behavior than prompt engineering can reach. On Emissary you start from a
base model, train it on examples of your task (or against a reward), and get back **checkpoints**
that you evaluate and then deploy for inference.

## How does post-training work on Emissary?

The lifecycle runs from a project to a live deployment:

```
project → dataset → training job → checkpoints → evaluation → deployment
```

1. **Create a training project**, a workspace that groups the jobs you run toward one goal.
2. **Upload a dataset** in the schema for your task type. Emissary profiles it and surfaces row
   counts, class balance, token-length distributions and outliers before you train.
3. **Start a training job** by choosing a base model, a technique and task type, your evaluation
   data and your hyperparameters. Most settings are prefilled.
4. **Compare checkpoints.** Each checkpoint is a snapshot of the model, typically one per epoch. A
   later checkpoint isn't always better, because training too long can overfit.
5. **Deploy the best checkpoint** on an inference engine and call it by name.

The concepts behind each step are defined on [Concepts](./concept), and the hands-on version is the
[post-training quickstart](./quickstart).

## What post-training techniques does Emissary support?

| | **SFT** (Supervised Fine-Tuning) | **GRPO** (Group Relative Policy Optimization) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How the model learns** | Reproduces the input-to-output pairs in your dataset | Improves against a reward signal by comparing several sampled completions per prompt |
| **Type of method** | Supervised learning | Reinforcement learning |
| **Task types** | Every fine-tunable task type | `text-generation` only |
| **Default?** | Yes | No |

Both techniques train **LoRA adapters**: small weight layers added on top of the frozen base model
rather than a rewrite of all its weights. That keeps training fast and makes checkpoints cheap to
serve, since many can share one base model on an engine. The knobs each technique exposes are in
[Hyperparameters](./parameters).

## What can I post-train a model to do?

A **task type** defines what your model does, and it shapes the dataset schema, the model's output
and the API you call once it's deployed. Supported task types include text `completion` and `chat`
(`text-generation`), `classification`, `regression`, `embedding`, `ner` (named-entity recognition),
and the vision tasks `clip-*` and `vlm-*`. See [Datasets](./datasets) for the schema of each.

## When should I post-train instead of prompting?

Prompting is the right first step for most tasks. Consider post-training when:

- You run the **same kind of task repeatedly** and want consistent, precise behavior.
- You have **examples of the right answer**, or a way to score answers that can serve as a reward.
- You want to serve a **smaller, purpose-built model** for a narrow job instead of a large general
  one.

Dataset quality is the single biggest lever on the quality of the result, so invest there first.

## How does post-training connect to routing and inference?

Post-training sits between the other two parts of the platform:

- **From routing.** Every routed request leaves a trajectory. Requests that no model in your
  catalog was confident about show where you have a capability gap, and the traffic itself is raw
  material for training a model of your own. See
  [from routing to your own models](../routing/insights#from-routing-to-your-own-models).
- **To inference.** A trained checkpoint serves nothing until you deploy it as a deployment on an
  [inference engine](../inference/what-is-llm-inference). Because fine-tunes are LoRA adapters,
  many checkpoints can share one engine.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to start?

A task you can describe, a **dataset** of examples in the schema for that task type, and a base
model that supports the task. JSONL is recommended for the most reliable dataset profiling, and
JSON and CSV are also supported.

### How do I know a model is good enough to deploy?

Provide a test dataset, either split from your training data or uploaded separately. Emissary runs
the model against it at every checkpoint, and **test functions** score each one. Compare checkpoints
on held-out scores, and watch for overfitting.

### Can I keep training a model I've already trained?

Yes. You can start a job from a published pre-trained model, or continue training from one of your
own checkpoints to refine it or adapt it to new data.
