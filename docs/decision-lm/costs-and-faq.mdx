# Costs and FAQ

## What does it cost to run?

- **Serverless:** **$0.05 per million tokens.**
- **Dedicated:** **$3/hr for models under 30B parameters.** Contact us for larger models. Bigger models are slower, so the effective cost on a 27B model is roughly **$0.15 per million tokens**, depending on your workload pattern. Steady, high-volume traffic that keeps the GPU busy lands at the low end.

Trained models are often cheaper to run than zero-shot ones. A zero-shot request carries every class description, while a trained model learns what the classes mean from your examples, so requests are shorter.

## What does it cost to train?

Training a classifier or regressor is cheap, and you can start small.

- **LoRA fine-tune, typical:** for 3-5 classes with about 100 samples each, training usually costs **under $15**.
- **LoRA fine-tune, larger datasets:** training on up to about 5,000 samples costs **under $50 at most**.

Start with the smaller dataset to confirm your labels and setup work, then scale up to more data if your held-out score is still improving.

## How can this be deployed?

- **Serverless on `Qwen3-8B`.** Pay per token with no engine to manage.
- **Dedicated for everything else.** Any other backbone runs on a dedicated engine.

Use **dedicated** when you have high throughput or a predefined time window, such as scheduled workflows. Use **serverless** when your workload is spiky, low-volume, or unpredictable. Autoscaling is also available on dedicated engines, so [reach out](mailto:support@withemissary.com) if you want it set up for your workload.

See [Inference concepts](/inference/concept), [Deployments](/inference/deployments), and [Autoscaling](/inference/autoscaling).

## Which base model should I pick?

Choose **Qwen3-8B** (not Qwen3-8B-Base) because it is the one you can deploy serverless.

- Go **bigger** if you need a smarter model, for example when the task is subtle and the smaller model's held-out score is still improving.
- Go **smaller** if your eval is already maxed out. A simpler task doesn't need a bigger model, and a smaller one is cheaper and faster.

Bigger and smaller backbones run on dedicated engines.

## Classification vs. regression: how do I choose?

Choose by the shape of your answer.

- Use **classification** when the answer is a choice among known options, such as route A or B, safe or unsafe, or which of five intents. You get a probability per class.
- Use **regression** when the answer is a point on a scale, such as urgency from 0 to 1, a quality score, or a sentiment intensity. You get a single number.

Both train and deploy the same way. The only difference is the loss function used in training, so a regressor is served from a classification inference engine just like a classifier.

If your labels are really ordered buckets (low, medium, high) and you have enough examples at a finer grain, regression often uses your data more efficiently. If you only ever act on a few discrete outcomes, classification is simpler to threshold and evaluate.
