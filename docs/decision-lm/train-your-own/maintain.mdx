# Maintain your model

A trained model is a snapshot of your data at one point in time. Plan to maintain it.

## Watch for drift

Inputs change over time, and the model's accuracy can quietly follow. Monitor two things:

- **Confidence.** If scores drift lower on live traffic, the inputs may have moved away from what the model was trained on.
- **Class frequency.** If the share of traffic in each class shifts, the world has changed, or the model has.

Log every decision with its input, probabilities, and chosen branch. Those logs are both your monitoring data and your next training set.

## Retrain

Retrain when drift shows up, when you have collected meaningfully more labeled data, or when you find a new category of error.

1. Label a sample of recent traffic, including the cases the model got wrong or was unsure about.
2. Add it to your training data and keep a fresh test set.
3. Train a new version and compare it to the deployed one on the same test set.
4. Deploy it alongside the current version and compare on live traffic before switching over. Checkpoints are yours, so you can version them and A/B test.

## Re-check thresholds after every retrain

Thresholds tuned on one version may not transfer to the next. Re-run your calibration check ([step 5 of the LoRA fine-tune](./lora-fine-tune#5-measure-on-your-own-hold-out-set)) and re-pick the cutoff each time.

## Plan for new base models

A LoRA adapter is trained on a specific base model. When you move to a newer base model, plan to retrain on the same dataset, so keep your datasets and test sets versioned alongside your checkpoints.
